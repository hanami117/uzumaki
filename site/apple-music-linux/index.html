Apple Music on Linux

Apple doesn’t make an Apple Music app for Linux. Uzumaki is a free, independent desktop client that does the job: your library, playlists and synced lyrics in a fast native window, with media keys and a terminal player.

Arch Linux (AUR) AppImage, any distro

Why there’s no Apple Music app on Linux

Apple makes Apple Music apps for macOS, Windows, iPhone and Android, but not for Linux. On Linux the official way to listen is music.apple.com in a browser tab, one tab among many, which stops when you close the browser.

Uzumaki is a real desktop app instead. It is written in Rust and draws its own interface on the GPU, so it opens quickly and scrolls smoothly even through a large library. It’s free and open source under the GPL, and it runs on Wayland and X11.

How it plays Apple Music

Uzumaki runs Apple’s own Apple Music web player in a hidden window, inside a standard licensed browser engine, and tells it what to play. You sign in on Apple’s own sign-in page, so Uzumaki never sees your password. Everything you see and click is Uzumaki’s own window.

Because the sound comes from Apple’s web player, the quality is what Apple offers on the web: 256 kbps AAC. Uzumaki streams the same way music.apple.com does and never saves or converts the music.

What you get

  • Synced lyrics, word by word. Lyrics fill in word by word when Apple has word timing, or line by line when it doesn’t. Read them full screen in Now Playing or in a side panel next to whatever you’re browsing.
  • AutoPlay and stations. When your queue, album or playlist ends, AutoPlay keeps going with similar songs, as in Apple’s apps. Create a station from any song or artist.
  • Animated artwork. Albums with a motion cover play it on the album page and in Now Playing (on Linux this uses ffmpeg). Always, Now Playing only, or off.
  • Your library, fast. Albums, artists, songs, playlists, Favourite Songs, New, Radio and Made for You. The library is kept on disk, so it opens at once and you can browse it offline. Create and edit playlists.
  • Local files in the same queue. FLAC, MP3, AAC/M4A, ALAC, Vorbis and WAV from your own music folders play inside the app, mixed with Apple Music songs. No subscription? Use Uzumaki as a local music player only.
  • Media keys and desktop widgets. Uzumaki registers with MPRIS, so media keys, the KDE and GNOME media widgets and playerctl control it. There’s also a tray icon, song-change notifications and hook scripts.
  • A terminal player, uzu. Control the running app from scripts and key bindings the way mpc controls mpd, or type uzu for a full-screen terminal player with your queue, playlists, search, albums and lyrics. It works over SSH too.
  • Themes and backgrounds. Dark, light or follow the system. Put Uzumaki’s swirl, your own picture or the playing cover behind the app, with dither, ASCII, halftone, scanline or blur effects. Every keyboard shortcut can be changed.

The home page has an interactive preview of the window and more screenshots.

Install on Arch Linux

Two packages are in the AUR. uzumaki-bin installs the prebuilt app and needs no build; uzumaki builds from source. Pick one: they conflict with each other. Both update with the rest of your system.

$ yay -S uzumaki-bin

Details, including what the source package builds: Uzumaki for Arch Linux.

Install on any other distro

Download the AppImage, make it executable and run it. One file, no install, no sudo. It needs glibc 2.35 or newer (Ubuntu 22.04 and later) and FUSE 2.

$ chmod +x Uzumaki-x86_64.AppImage $ ./Uzumaki-x86_64.AppImage

You can also build it from source with Rust and Node.js. Both ways: Uzumaki for Linux, any distro.

Also on macOS

The same app runs on Macs with Apple silicon and macOS 13 or later, from a disk image or with Homebrew. See Uzumaki for macOS.

$ brew install --cask hanami117/uzumaki/uzumaki

What you need

  • An Apple Music subscription, to stream. Your own music files play without one.
  • A 64-bit Intel or AMD PC (x86_64) with a GPU that has Vulkan drivers, on Wayland or X11.
  • Songs stream at 256 kbps AAC, with a 64 kbps data saver. Lossless and Dolby Atmos aren’t available this way.

Questions

Is Uzumaki an official Apple Music app?

No. Uzumaki is an independent project and is not made, affiliated with or endorsed by Apple. It plays music through Apple’s own web player, with your own Apple Music account.

Is it free?

Yes. Uzumaki is free and open source under the GPL-3.0. To stream Apple Music you need your own Apple Music subscription; your own music files play without one.

Does it play lossless or Dolby Atmos?

No. Apple offers 256 kbps AAC to its web player, which is the player Uzumaki uses. Lossless and Spatial Audio need Apple’s own apps, which don’t exist on Linux.

Which distros does it run on?

Arch Linux and its derivatives through the AUR, and most other x86_64 distros through the AppImage (glibc 2.35 or newer: Ubuntu 22.04 and later, Debian 12 and later, current Fedora, openSUSE Tumbleweed and so on). Wayland and X11 both work.

Can I download songs to keep?

No. Uzumaki streams Apple Music the same way music.apple.com does and never saves or converts the audio. Songs you own as files play from your own music folders.
