01 · native

A music app that feels like it belongs on your desktop

No browser tab. Uzumaki draws its own interface on the GPU and uses Apple's own web player, hidden in the background, for the actual playback.

  • Native and GPU‑rendered

    Written in Rust and drawn with the GPU (Vulkan on Linux). A sidebar like Apple's, pins, a collapsible rail, sortable song tables that adapt to the window width, drag and drop, and keyboard shortcuts you can change.

  • Synced lyrics, word by word

    Lyrics glide along with the song and fill in word by word when Apple has word timing, or line by line when it doesn't. Read them full screen, or in a panel beside whatever you're browsing.

  • Now Playing, with backgrounds

    A full-screen Now Playing view with the cover, controls and lyrics. Set a background behind the app or behind Now Playing: the built-in swirl, your own picture or the current cover, with dither, ASCII, halftone, scanline or blur effects.

  • Your own files, in the same queue

    Point it at your music folders. FLAC, MP3, AAC/M4A, ALAC, Vorbis and WAV play inside the app and mix with Apple Music songs in one queue. Local files have their own page and search tab.

  • Media keys, tray and notifications

    MPRIS on Linux and Now Playing on macOS, so media keys and desktop widgets just work. A tray icon with playback controls, optional close-to-tray, song-change notifications, and hook scripts that run on every song change.

  • Your whole library, fast

    Library, albums, artists, playlists, Favourite Songs, New, Radio and Made for You. The library is kept on disk, so it opens at once and you can browse it offline. Create and edit playlists, mark favourites, and pick light or dark.

02 · make it yours

Your picture, your theme, your keys

Behind the app you can have Uzumaki’s own swirl, a picture of your choice or the cover of whatever is playing, redrawn with an effect. The window next to this text is the real thing: it runs the app’s own effect code, right in your browser.

  • Source. The built-in swirl, your own picture (PNG or JPEG) or the playing song’s cover, or nothing at all.
  • Effect. None, Dither, ASCII, Halftone, Scanlines or Blur.
  • Strength. From faint to strong, never fully opaque, with a separate strength for song lists.
  • Fade. The picture can fade out towards the bottom, so long lists sit on the plain background.
  • Where. On every page, or only on card pages such as Home and Radio.

Now Playing has a background of its own: a gradient in the cover’s colour, the cover itself with its own effect under a dark scrim, or the same as the rest of the app.

Dark, light, or your system

Pick Dark or Light, or let Uzumaki follow your computer’s appearance, which is the default. The backgrounds follow the theme: the same effects draw as light on black in Dark and as ink on near-white paper in Light.

Your shortcuts

Every shortcut can be rebound in Settings › Keyboard: click one, then press the new keys. Esc cancels and Backspace removes it, and a combination another shortcut already has moves over to the one you’re setting.

The number keys 1–9 can use Ctrl or Alt, or be switched off, and open the main pages or your sidebar pins. The shortcuts tour can be played again from Settings.

  • Play or pauseSpace
  • Next songCtrl→
  • Shuffle on or offCtrlS
  • Show or hide the sidebarCtrlB
  • Lyrics in the right sidebarCtrlL
  • Up Next in the right sidebarCtrlU
  • Full-screen Now PlayingCtrlShiftF
  • Keyboard shortcutsCtrl/
  • Number keys: the main pages, or your sidebar pinsCtrl1to9

The defaults, as shown on Linux; on a Mac, Ctrl is Cmd.

03 · how it works

From download to playing in four steps

  1. 01

    Install

    On Arch, from the AUR; on other Linux, as an AppImage or from source; on macOS, the DMG or Homebrew. Version 0.1.0 is out for macOS and Linux: see Download.

  2. 02

    Sign in, or go local

    Sign in with Apple Music on Apple's own sign-in page. Uzumaki never sees your password. No subscription? Choose local files only and pick your music folders instead.

  3. 03

    Play

    Open a song, an album or a playlist. Apple Music songs and your own files share one queue, with synced lyrics, media keys and a tray icon.

  4. 04

    Control it from the terminal

    Script it, bind it to keys, or type uzu for a full-screen player.

    $ uzu play "late night" $ uzu next
$ uzu

Built for the terminal too: uzu

uzu controls the running app the way mpc controls mpd. Use it in scripts and key bindings, or just type uzu for a full-screen terminal player with your queue, playlists, search, albums and lyrics. It even works over SSH.

04 · requirements

What you need

An Apple Music subscription, for streaming

Uzumaki is a different way to use the subscription you already have. You sign in once with your Apple Account, in Apple's own sign-in page. Your own music files play without one.

Apple's standard streaming quality

Songs stream at 256 kbps AAC, the quality Apple offers on the web. Lossless and Dolby Atmos aren't available this way. There's also a 64 kbps data saver.

Linux or macOS

On Linux, a GPU with Vulkan drivers, on Wayland or X11. On macOS 13 or later with Apple silicon it runs as a regular app, with media keys, a menu bar icon and notifications.

05 · download

Get Uzumaki

The first public beta, version 0.1.0, is out for macOS and Linux. Free and open source under the GPL. Each system has an install page.

macOS

macOS 13 or later, Apple silicon. Open the disk image and drag Uzumaki to Applications.

Download for macOS

First launch: Uzumaki isn’t notarized by Apple yet, so macOS stops it the first time. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down and click Open Anyway. Once is enough.

Or with Homebrew:

$ brew install --cask hanami117/uzumaki/uzumaki macOS install guide →

Arch Linux

From the AUR, with two packages. uzumaki-bin is the quick install: it uses the prebuilt AppImage. uzumaki builds from source. Both update with the rest of your system.

$ yay -S uzumaki-bin $ yay -S uzumaki

Pick one: the two packages conflict with each other.

Arch install guide →

AppImage · any distro

One file for any Linux distro on x86_64. Make it executable and run it. No install, no sudo.

Download the AppImage $ chmod +x Uzumaki-x86_64.AppImage $ ./Uzumaki-x86_64.AppImage

Needs FUSE 2 (libfuse2 on Debian and Ubuntu, fuse2 on Arch), or start it with --appimage-extract-and-run. glibc 2.35 or newer (Ubuntu 22.04 and later).

Or build it from source: needs Rust, Node.js and npm.

06 · faq

Questions

How does playback work?

Uzumaki runs Apple's own Apple Music web player in a hidden window and tells it what to play. The audio is played by that official player, inside a standard licensed browser engine. Uzumaki's own window shows everything else.

Can I download songs or keep them after my subscription ends?

No. Uzumaki streams Apple Music the same way music.apple.com does and never saves or converts the audio. Songs you own as files play from your own music folders.

Is it official?

No. Uzumaki is an independent project and is not made, affiliated with or endorsed by Apple.

Why not lossless?

Apple only offers 256 kbps AAC to its web player, and that is the player Uzumaki uses. Lossless and Spatial Audio need Apple's own apps.

Is my Apple password stored by Uzumaki?

No. You sign in on Apple's own page in a separate window, and the login stays in that player's profile on your computer, just like signing in to a browser. Settings → Account has a Sign out button that removes it.

Do lyrics work for every song?

Lyrics come from Apple Music, so they show when Apple has them. Many songs are timed word by word, others line by line, and some have plain lyrics only. Local files don't have lyrics yet.

Do media keys and desktop widgets work?

Yes. On Linux Uzumaki registers with MPRIS, so media keys, KDE and GNOME widgets and tools like playerctl see it, whether an Apple Music song or a local file is playing. On macOS it uses the system's Now Playing controls.

When can I download it?

Now: version 0.1.0 is out for macOS and Linux (AUR and AppImage). The Download section has an install page for each system, and releases appear on the GitHub releases page.

How do I update?

Uzumaki tells you when a new version is out. With the AUR or Homebrew, update as you update everything else (yay, brew upgrade). With an AppImage, download the new file and replace the old one. From source, run ./install.sh again.

Is there a Windows version?

Not yet. It’s planned. See Windows.
